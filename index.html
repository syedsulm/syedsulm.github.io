“BASIC” Underwater Acoustic Deterrent System — US Army Corps of Engineers (ERDC)

Project Lead · $340K award · UW × ERDC × Virginia Tech · 2024–present
▾
Problem

Invasive silver and bighead carp are advancing through the Mississippi River system toward the Great Lakes. Navigation locks are the choke points where they can be stopped, but the acoustic field inside one — hard-walled, reverberant, bubble-laden, and strongly sheared — is poorly predicted by free-field models. Worse, the through-flow generates its own broadband noise; if that self-noise overlaps the deterrent band, the barrier is masked by the very structure it protects.

Method
  • Built a 3D RANS model of the lock approach, dogleg contraction and chamber, solved in COMSOL and independently in OpenFOAM so no conclusion rests on a single code.
  • Ran an 11-case grid convergence study from 4M to 76M cells, sweeping inflation-layer count and first-layer thickness to place y+ deliberately across wall-function and resolved-sublayer regimes; benchmarked against the universal law of the wall, Coles' law of the wake, and the velocity-defect law.
  • Applied a Stochastic Noise Generation and Radiation (SNGR) method to synthesize a divergence-free turbulent velocity field from the steady RANS k and ε, then evaluated the Lighthill turbulence source tensor — recovering unsteady acoustic source information from a steady solve at a fraction of LES cost.
  • Sampled 10,000 probe points through the chamber and assembled spatial maps of broadband source level; executed on an HPC cluster under SLURM with multigrid solvers and domain decomposition.
Impact

Delivered the operational BASIC system to ERDC, replacing empirical siting with a physics-based specification. Source maps show broadband Lighthill levels of 139–143 dB re 1 µPa concentrated near the dogleg shear layer, and a source spectrum peaking at 1.22 Hz — well below the carp hearing band. That separation is the design-enabling result: deterrent signals can be placed in a frequency window the lock's own hydrodynamic noise does not mask.

Turbulent Kinetic Energy Budget Closure in Multiphase Sprays

Research Assistant · PI: Dr. John Palmore · CAMP-PhyRE Lab, UW
▾
Problem

Engineering-grade multiphase spray models close the turbulence budget with isotropic assumptions and gradient-diffusion hypotheses. Near the liquid core and in the bubble-laden near field these assumptions break down: production, transport, and dissipation of turbulent kinetic energy are strongly anisotropic, and the literature offers no resolved budget to calibrate against.

Method
  • Resolved the full anisotropic Reynolds stress tensor from high-fidelity multiphase simulation data rather than assuming isotropy.
  • Computed third-order velocity moments to evaluate turbulent transport terms directly, closing every term of the TKE budget (production, transport, pressure–strain, dissipation) instead of lumping them into a model constant.
  • Quantified interfacial energy exchange between phases to identify where dispersed-phase feedback dominates the budget.
  • Built the analysis as a reproducible post-processing pipeline over large time-resolved fields on cluster storage.
Impact

Produced term-by-term budget data that fills a documented gap in multiphase spray modeling and provides a calibration target for RANS/LES closures — the physical basis underpinning the acoustic deterrent system delivered to ERDC.

Model-Predictive Attitude Control for Post-Capture Spacecraft

ME 569 — Data-Driven Control & Machine Learning · TA under Dr. Steven L. Brunton
▾
Problem

After capturing irregular orbital debris, a servicing spacecraft's inertia tensor changes abruptly and unpredictably, and the combined body tumbles. Classical fixed-gain attitude control degrades under this plant change, and naive controllers command torques the reaction wheels cannot deliver — saturation then destabilizes the recovery maneuver.

Method
  • Formulated rigid-body attitude dynamics (quaternion kinematics + Euler's equations) and linearized about the operating point for a receding-horizon MPC problem.
  • Encoded actuator saturation as hard input constraints and rate limits as slew constraints — the reason MPC is chosen over LQR here — with disturbance terms for residual debris dynamics.
  • Tuned state/input weighting and horizon length against settling time versus control effort trade-offs; solved the constrained QP at each step.
  • Validated across three deliberately distinct regimes: large pointing-error slew, pure de-tumbling, and combined displacement-plus-rate recovery.
Impact

Controller re-acquired the target attitude in all three scenarios without violating actuator limits, demonstrating a constraint-aware alternative to gain-scheduled control for on-orbit servicing and active debris removal.

Data-Driven Flight Dynamics: SINDy Reduced-Order Models inside Real-Time MPC

Data-Driven Control & ML · JSBSim 6-DoF · CAMP-PhyRE Lab
▾
Problem

High-agility flight leaves the linear envelope where standard aero look-up tables are valid, and full 6-DoF nonlinear models are too expensive to evaluate inside an onboard optimizer. Guidance therefore either sacrifices agility or misses real-time deadlines. A black-box neural surrogate would solve the cost problem while creating a worse one: a flight-dynamics engineer cannot inspect it, and it cannot be certified.

Method
  • Generated aggressive-maneuver datasets in JSBSim (6-DoF) with excitation designed to persistently excite coupled longitudinal–lateral modes.
  • Applied SINDy (sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics) over a polynomial/trigonometric candidate library with sparsity-promoting regression, recovering interpretable state-space equations rather than a black-box net.
  • Verified that identified terms correspond to physically meaningful aerodynamic cross-couplings (e.g. roll–yaw and angle-of-attack dependence), not fitting artifacts; cross-validated on held-out maneuvers.
  • Embedded the sparse model as the prediction model in an MPC trajectory-tracking loop, so each horizon evaluation costs a handful of terms instead of a full-fidelity integration.
Impact

Achieved real-time-capable, high-agility trajectory tracking with a model compact enough for onboard guidance and transparent enough to be inspected by a flight-dynamics engineer — a practical route to certifiable data-driven control.

RL–MPC Hybrid Control & Sim-to-Real Pipeline for a FANUC CRX Collaborative Arm

CAMP-PhyRE Lab · Reinforcement learning + optimal control
▾
Problem

Reinforcement learning explores well but offers no safety or constraint guarantees on real hardware; MPC guarantees constraint satisfaction but depends on a model that never fully matches the physical arm. Policies trained purely in simulation also collapse on deployment because of the reality gap.

Method
  • Built a hybrid architecture: an RL policy proposes task-level actions while an MPC layer enforces joint limits, velocity bounds, and workspace constraints before anything reaches the controller.
  • Trained policies in simulation with domain randomization over inertial parameters, friction, and latency to make them robust to model mismatch.
  • Designed the sim-to-real transfer pipeline — consistent state/action interfaces, rate matching, and staged deployment from simulator to hardware.
  • Instrumented tracking error and constraint-violation metrics to compare pure-RL, pure-MPC, and hybrid baselines.
Impact

Demonstrated learned manipulation behavior that remains constraint-feasible on real collaborative hardware — the combination industrial deployments actually require, where an unguarded learned policy is not acceptable.

Distributed Multi-Agent Coordination with Control-Barrier-Function Safety

Supervised student teams · Crazyflie quadrotors + ground robots
▾
Problem

Heterogeneous teams of aerial and ground robots must hold formation and avoid each other and obstacles using only local information — centralized planning does not scale and learned policies alone cannot certify collision avoidance.

Method
  • Implemented distributed formation control with neighbor-only communication so behavior scales with team size.
  • Layered a control barrier function quadratic program (CBF-QP) as a minimally invasive safety filter: it modifies the nominal command only when the forward-invariance condition on the safe set would be violated.
  • Trained RL policies for the nominal navigation task and let the CBF-QP certify the output, combining performance with a formal safety guarantee.
  • Validated in simulation and on Crazyflie-class quadrotors with ground robots in cluttered indoor settings.
Impact

Collision-free formation keeping and navigation on real hardware; the safety-filter pattern was reused across multiple student projects as a standard architecture, and mentoring these teams built the supervision experience I bring to collaborative R&D.

Remaining Useful Life Prediction for Engine Prognostics & Health Management

Center of Advanced Research in Engineering & AI, NUST · NASA C-MAPSS · Submitted manuscript
▾
Problem

Turbofan maintenance scheduled on fixed intervals either replaces healthy components early or misses degrading ones. Data-driven remaining-useful-life prediction enables condition-based maintenance instead, but deep models are notoriously sensitive to hyperparameters — particularly the sliding-window length, which sets how much degradation history the network can see. Hand-tuned networks report good average error while degrading badly on the engines nearest end of life.

Method
  • Built a convolutional network over multivariate sensor time series from NASA C-MAPSS — two convolution + ReLU stages (64 then 32 filters, kernels 3/4/2), pooling, flattening, and fully connected regression to scalar RUL. Convolution is the right inductive bias: degradation signatures are local temporal patterns recurring at unknown offsets.
  • Wrapped training in a multi-objective optimization framework tuning window length, kernel size, batch size, epochs and steps-per-epoch together — these interact, so tuning them independently finds a false optimum.
  • Ran a data-visualisation stage first, tracking sensor channels (e.g. HPC outlet temperature) against RUL to confirm which channels carry genuine degradation signal before committing them to the model.
  • Evaluated per-engine predicted-versus-actual RUL trajectories rather than aggregate error alone, so late-life divergence could not hide inside a good mean score.
Impact

Optimization markedly tightened prediction against ground truth, with the largest gains in the final cycles before failure — precisely the region that determines whether a maintenance decision is actionable.

Aircraft Weapons Bay Design Optimization

National Aerospace Science and Technology Park (NASTP) · Cavity aeroacoustics & store separation
▾
Problem

An open weapons bay in flight is an aerodynamic cavity: the shear layer spanning the opening drives intense resonant pressure oscillations that fatigue structure, shake avionics, and create an unsteady flow field that makes store release unpredictable. The existing bay used straight internal collars, which do little to disrupt that resonance.

Method
  • Established solver credibility first on the lid-driven cavity benchmark — a 4:1 aspect-ratio case with published horizontal and vertical velocity-component profiles — before trusting any result on the real geometry.
  • Modelled the bay in ANSYS Fluent, extracting static pressure fields and velocity streamlines to locate the primary recirculation and the shear-layer impingement point on the aft wall, which is where cavity resonance is driven.
  • Ran a parametric comparison of collar geometries — straight (baseline) against backward-leaned and yawed collars — on the reasoning that leaning and yawing thickens and de-correlates the shear layer, breaking the coherent feedback loop that sustains resonance rather than merely deflecting flow.
Impact

Produced a benchmarked optimised bay configuration with weakened cavity recirculation and reduced aft-wall pressure loading relative to the straight-collar baseline, supporting quieter carriage and more predictable store separation.

Aerodynamic Optimization of a Formula Student Racing Vehicle Shell

Formula Electric Club · High Performance Computing Research Lab
▾
Problem

The existing bodyshell measured Cd = 0.5. On an energy-limited electric Formula Student car, drag is a direct tax on range and top speed, and the target set for the redesign was Cd = 0.32 — a 36% reduction unreachable by smoothing surfaces alone. The shell also had to wrap an existing tubular spaceframe whose hardpoints were fixed.

Method
  • Built the shell as a lofted surface through a series of parallel section planes referenced to the spaceframe, so body sections became explicit design variables and the packaging constraint was satisfied by construction rather than checked afterwards.
  • Ran external aerodynamic simulation in ANSYS Fluent on HPC resources, decomposing the force into pressure and viscous components separately — the decisive diagnostic, since pressure-dominated drag calls for changing the rear closure while viscous-dominated drag calls for reducing wetted area, and treating them as one number leads to reshaping the wrong end of the car.
  • Iterated the aft body taper against the stagnation and separation behaviour visible in the surface pressure distribution, working the section profiles rather than local fairings.
Impact

Established a parametric, section-driven aero workflow that ties every shape change back to a measurable force component and can be re-run for each season's chassis, replacing intuition-led body styling with a quantified drag-reduction path.

Formula SAE Structural Program — Chassis, Suspension & Steering

Design & CFD Intern · Formula SAE, Virginia Tech · 2023
▾
Problem

Lap time is mass-sensitive, but the accessible mass sits at welded chassis joints — exactly where stress concentrations and crash-structure requirements make naive material removal dangerous. Carried-over suspension geometry was neither weight-optimal nor matched to the current mass distribution, and the steering assembly sits high and far forward, poor for both mass and yaw inertia.

Method
  • Ran density-based topology optimization on critical weld-joint brackets with a compliance objective under a volume-fraction constraint and prescribed bump, braking and cornering load cases, then re-interpreted the optimiser output into weldable, machinable geometry and re-verified it by FEA.
  • Wrote custom MATLAB scripts deriving suspension kinematics from first principles — A-arm hardpoint placement, roll-centre migration, camber and toe curves, and load transfer — so every design variable stayed inspectable and could be swept.
  • Traced the load path from grip to column on the steering assembly and removed material only where it carried no steering torque, with the ergonomic and rulebook packaging envelope fixed as a constraint at the start.
  • Validated by FEA under steering-torque loading against von Mises limits and an explicit deflection ceiling set to preserve feel.
Impact

10% mass reduction at critical weld joints with the freed budget redirected into driver protection (+5%); 6% suspension subsystem weight reduction with a reusable parametric toolchain; 25% steering structural mass reduction with stiffness preserved.

Fixed-Wing VTOL & Autonomous Quadcopter UAVs — CFD/Wind-Tunnel Campaign

Design lead · Hokie Flying Club, Virginia Tech · Concept → flying prototype
▾
Problem

VTOL aircraft must be aerodynamically efficient in cruise while carrying the structural and propulsion penalty of vertical flight, all under hard weight and factor-of-safety limits. Compounding this, each CFD case was being set up by hand, so the team could evaluate only a fraction of the configurations it wanted to.

Method
  • Ran CFD campaigns in ANSYS Fluent across the configuration space (fuselage surfacing, wing–rotor interaction, transition attitudes) and cross-checked against wind-tunnel measurements.
  • Standardized meshing, boundary conditions, and post-processing into a templated workflow — the change that cut simulation setup time 15–20%.
  • Designed compact fuselage surfacing for integrated electronics and battery-locking mechanisms specified with GD&T for repeatable assembly.
  • Built 3D-printed prototypes with LiDAR/TOF sensors for autonomy testing and verified structure with FEA against weight and FoS targets.
Impact

Delivered flying VTOL and autonomous quadcopter prototypes end to end while cutting simulation turnaround 15–20%, letting the team explore more of the design space within the same season.

Design for Development — Crop Harvester & Manual Hydraulic Brick Press

Lead mechanical designer · Appropriate-technology design study · Concept → CAD → prototype documentation
▾
Problem

Two machines for smallholder and community-scale users where commercial equipment is economically or infrastructurally impossible: a harvester for 1–5 hectare plots that a farming cooperative could own collectively and repair with local welding, and a compressed-earth block press requiring no electricity at all, since delivered brick cost is dominated by transport on remote sites.

Method
  • Harvester — architecture chosen for repairability, not performance. A ground-engaging lifting deck feeds an inclined belt-and-cleat elevator discharging into a trailing collection bin. Cleat pitch and belt inclination were sized against the static friction angle of the harvested material so product is carried rather than rolling back, with belt speed matched to ground speed to avoid bunching at the throat and throw at the discharge. Welded box-section chassis analysed in SolidWorks under combined towing draft, deck reaction and a struck-obstruction shock case.
  • Brick press — human-powered hydraulic force multiplication. A hand lever drives a small-bore pump cylinder feeding a large-bore ram; the design reduces to choosing the lever-arm and piston-area ratios such that one operator of ordinary strength reaches CSEB compaction pressure (order 2–4 MPa) at sustainable effort. A direct mechanical toggle press cannot reach that pressure by hand at usable block sizes.
  • Deflection, not yield, governed the mould. Mould walls and top platen were checked for wall deflection under full compaction pressure — a mould that bows produces barrel-shaped blocks that will not course properly.
  • Both machines detailed for manual (non-CNC) fabrication from locally stocked section, with a shear-pin sacrificial coupling on the harvester drivetrain and lever-driven ejection integrated into the press cycle.
Impact

Design-stage modelling projects a substantial reduction in labour-hours per hectare for the harvester and a large materials-cost saving against delivered fired brick for the press, with the wider intent of shifting production from distant suppliers to the community doing the building.
